# 17 - NLP metrics translated

---

Here are a few metrics that are useful to know about for the exam.

You don't need to be an expert in these methods, but being aware of what they are can help set context if they crop up in questions.

WER, Word Error Rate, is a widely used metric for evaluating the performance of speech recognition and natural language processing systems.

WER measures how accurately a system transcribed spoken language into text by comparing the system's output to a reference transcript.

If you see the acronym ASR, it means Automatic Speech Recognition.

WER is the standard metric for evaluating how accurately automatic speech recognition systems convert spoken language into text.

It helps compare the performance of different models or systems under similar conditions.

Lower WER indicates better performance, but it's important to interpret WER alongside other metrics, especially in noisy or multilingual environments.

Let's review the common NLP metrics.

Accuracy is useful for balanced classification problems, but can be misleading in imbalanced data sets.

Precision, Recall, and F1 score are essential for classification tasks, especially in cases of class imbalance, e.g. spam detection.

BLU, bilingual evaluation understudy, and RUGE, Recall-oriented understudy for gistening evaluation, are widely used for machine translation and text summarization, respectively, measuring similarity between generated and reference texts.

Poplexity evaluates language models with lower values indicating better predictions of word sequences.

WER, word error, rate is crucial for speech recognition, assessing transcription accuracy.

Embedding based metrics like co-sene similarity and bird score measure semantic similarity in text generation tasks.

Choosing the right metric depends on whether the goal is accuracy, recall, fluency, or contextual understanding.
