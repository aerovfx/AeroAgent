Natural language processing, or NLP, is a field of artificial intelligence that enables

machines to comprehend and interact with human language, both spoken and written.

It bridges the gap between human communication and machine understanding, allowing computers

to process, analyze, and generate text or speech in a way that feels natural.

NLP is the driving force behind technologies like chatbots, virtual assistants, and language

translation tools.

It transforms complex human language into a structured format that machines can interpret

and respond to effectively.

Tokenization This involves breaking down text into smaller units, such as words, sub words,

or characters.

For example, the sentence, its raining today, can be tokenized as it, S, raining, today,

enabling the model to process each element individually.

Passing analyzes the grammatical structure of sentences to uncover relationships between

words and phrases.

Example Passing helps identify the subject, verb, and object in the cat chase the mouse,

meaning it clear who did what.

Sentiment analysis determines the emotional tone or polarity of a piece of text, whether

it's positive, negative, or neutral.

Example A review stating, this product is amazing would be classified as positive sentiment.

NLP is essential for enabling machines to understand the complexities and subtleties

of human language.

It powers large language models, LLMs, by allowing them to grasp contextual nuances,

generate coherent and contextually appropriate responses, perform tasks like language, translation,

text summarisation, and question answering.

Without NLP, the seamless interactions we experience with AI-powered tools like voice assistance

and chatbots wouldn't be possible.

It's the foundational technology that makes human machine communication intuitive and efficient.

Large language modelling, predicting the future of text.

Language modelling forms the backbone of how large language models, LLMs, function.

It involves teaching a model to understand the probability distribution of words, enabling

it to predict the next word in a sentence.

This seemingly simple task has profound implications as it underpins the generation of coherent,

contextually accurate text.

The ability to predict the next word allows LLMs to create text that mimics human language,

making them invaluable for tasks such as text summarisation, compressing lengthy articles

into concise summaries.

Translation, converting text seamlessly between languages, conversational agents, powering

chatbots that can respond naturally and informatively.

Key concepts, auto-regressive models predict data sequences, ensuring coherence in text

or audio generation, transformer based architectures, the backbone of tools like GPT, Excel, in tasks

requiring context understanding.

Some such as embeddings and vectorisation are crucial in data representation.

Tokenisation, the gateway to understanding text.

Tokenisation is the crucial first step in processing text for LLMs.

It involves breaking a sentence into smaller units called tokens which can range from

individual characters to full words or sub words.

How tokenisation works.

A simple sentence like the developer's favourite machine can be tokenised in two ways.

By spaces, by spaces and punctuation.

Advanced tokenisation, modern models often use sub word tokenisation, breaking words into

smaller meaningful units.

This method helps the model understand word structures without requiring separate entries for

variations.

A token dictionary efficiently encodes each token for model processing.

Embeddings, translating words into numbers.

Once tokenised, text must be converted into a form machines can process.

This is where embeddings come in.

Embeddings represent tokens as numerical vectors that capture their meaning and relationships.

How it works.

Tokenisation works with similar meanings are assigned vectors that are close in multidimensional

space.

For example, happy has the numbers 1.2, 0.5 and joyful has 1.1, 0.6.

On the other hand, car has 4.0, 3.5.

Here, happy and joyful are closer because they share semantic meaning, while car is farther

away.

Embeddings embeddings.

Initially, embeddings are random.

Through training on massive data sets, the model adjusts these vectors, creating a nuanced

understanding of language.

Once text is vectorised, it is stored in a vector database.
